\begin{abstract}
Contactless human-robot interaction (HRI) enables operators to direct industrial arms without diverting their hands from critical manual tasks. However, connecting multimodal perception (voice and vision) to proprietary industrial robot controllers often causes communication delays, complex calibration steps, and tedious manual testing. In this paper, we present a deterministic multimodal framework for color-coded material handling using ROS~2 and a 6-DOF KUKA KR6 R900-2 industrial manipulator. The system combines an offline speech recognition engine (Vosk) with phonetic alias mapping, a 2D planar homography calibration using a 12-marker ArUco constellation, and MoveIt~2 motion planning with the Pilz Industrial Motion Planner. Motion trajectories and robot telemetry are exchanged with the KUKA KRC4 controller over standard TCP/IP using the Ethernet KRL Interface (EKI) on port 54600. Furthermore, we implement an automated benchmarking pipeline that eliminates manual script triggering and coordinate logging. Physical testing on the hardware testbed achieves a mean decision latency of \SI{1.26}{\second}, an average Cartesian position error of \SI{3.18}{\milli\meter} ($\pm\SI{0.33}{\milli\meter}$), a mean joint tracking error of $1.55^\circ$ ($\pm 0.11^\circ$), and a \SI{100.0}{\percent} pick-and-place success rate across physical baseline trials. This framework provides an accessible, low-compute solution for reliable hands-free collaboration on commercial industrial robots.

\keywords{Human-Robot Interaction \and ROS 2 \and Industrial Manipulator \and KUKA EKI \and Multimodal Perception \and Automated Benchmarking \and Planar Homography \and MoveIt 2.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

In modern assembly lines, packaging cells, and medical assistance workstations, voice and vision interfaces allow operators to guide robotic arms without physical contact~\cite{quirubot2016,schafer2023smart}. While lightweight collaborative robots (cobots) are popular, rigid industrial manipulators—such as the KUKA KR6 Agilus series—remain essential in production due to their superior rigidity, high speed, and high repeatability ($\pm\SI{0.03}{\milli\meter}$).

However, integrating commercial industrial controllers (such as KUKA KRC4) with modern ROS~2 multimodal AI pipelines faces three major practical challenges:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Proprietary Controller Isolation}: Industrial controllers run closed operating systems designed for fixed scripts rather than dynamic sensor feedback.
    \item \textbf{Network Latency}: Standard fieldbus options like KUKA Fast Robot Interface (FRI) are costly and limited to specific research robots~\cite{lbr_stack2024}. Standard robots rely on Ethernet KRL Interface (EKI), which introduces \SI{12}{\milli\second} to \SI{50}{\milli\second} communication delays that require stable trajectory planning~\cite{kuka_eki_eval2022}.
    \item \textbf{Manual Testing Overhead}: Evaluating robotic pick-and-place typically requires operators to manually capture camera images, calculate coordinates, type terminal commands, and pause the arm to measure errors. This slows down testing and introduces human measurement error.
\end{enumerate}

To solve these issues, this paper introduces a streamlined, deterministic multimodal manipulation pipeline and an automated benchmarking runner for color-coded material handling using ROS~2 and a KUKA KR6 R900-2 industrial robot. The key contributions of this paper are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Modular Multimodal Architecture}: An integrated ROS~2 stack combining on-device offline voice recognition (Vosk), ArUco homography vision, and MoveIt~2 planning connected to a real KUKA KRC4 controller via EKI over TCP/IP.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Motion Control}: Implementation of the Pilz Industrial Motion Planner for smooth linear (LIN) and point-to-point (PTP) motions with tool center point (TCP) offset compensation ($Z_{\text{offset}} = \SI{76}{\milli\meter}$) and pneumatic dwell timing (\SI{500}{\milli\second} vacuum buildup, \SI{400}{\milli\second} venting).
    \item \textbf{Automated Benchmarking Workflow}: A dedicated testing node that automatically manages the full experimental cycle—querying vision, sending motion goals, logging joint errors, and calculating spatial precision.
    \item \textbf{Physical Hardware Validation}: Empirical validation on the physical KUKA robot testbed proving sub-\SI{3.5}{\milli\meter} accuracy, \SI{1.26}{\second} decision latency, and \SI{100}{\percent} baseline task success.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Industrial Robot Interfacing in ROS}
Connecting external software to industrial manipulators is a key topic in robotics research. Sanfilippo et al.~\cite{jopenshowvar2015} introduced JOpenShowVar to read and write KUKA controller variables over TCP/IP. Kunz et al.~\cite{lbr_stack2024} created the LBR-Stack, providing \texttt{ros2\_control} drivers for KUKA LBR research manipulators via Fast Robot Interface (FRI). For standard industrial robots (Agilus, Cybertech), Ethernet KRL Interface (EKI) is the standard Ethernet interface. Vargas et al.~\cite{kuka_eki_eval2022} demonstrated that combining EKI with client-side trajectory planning in ROS delivers reliable, sensor-guided manipulation.

\subsection{Multimodal Voice and Vision Interaction}
Voice-guided robot assistants have been developed for surgical and industrial assembly tasks. Mendoza-Larios et al.~\cite{quirubot2016} built Quirubot, combining speech recognition and image processing for surgical tool handover. Schäfer et al.~\cite{schafer2023smart} created a multimodal assistant using speech and machine vision for tool tracking. Radhakrishnan et al.~\cite{voice_ros2_2024} evaluated voice-controlled manipulation in ROS~2, noting that cloud speech systems often suffer from network delay and noise. In our work, we use an offline Kaldi-based Vosk engine with phonetic alias dictionaries, ensuring fast local execution without cloud dependency.

\section{System Architecture and Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

The system consists of five integrated layers: (1) Hardware and Network Interface, (2) Speech-to-Intent Pipeline, (3) Perspective-Corrected Vision Engine, (4) Motion Planning Layer, and (5) Automated Benchmark Orchestrator.

\subsection{Hardware Setup and Network Communication}
The physical testbed uses a 6-DOF KUKA KR6 R900-2 sixx Agilus industrial robot (payload: \SI{6}{\kilogram}, reach: \SI{901}{\milli\meter}, repeatability: $\pm\SI{0.03}{\milli\meter}$) with a KUKA KRC4 compact controller running KSS 8.3. A custom vacuum gripper is mounted at the tool flange (\texttt{tool0}). The suction cup contact point defines the active Tool Center Point (TCP) with a calibrated vertical offset:
\begin{equation}
    Z_{\text{offset}} = L_{\text{base}} + L_{\text{bellows}} = \SI{60}{\milli\meter} + \SI{16}{\milli\meter} = \SI{0.076}{\meter}
\end{equation}

The robot connects to the ROS~2 workstation over a dedicated Gigabit Ethernet cable (Robot IP: \texttt{192.168.1.147}, Port: \texttt{54600}). The KRC4 controller runs a KRL program (\texttt{ros\_eki.src}) exchanging XML telegrams. Gripper actuation is sent via the \texttt{/gripper\_cmd} topic, using a \SI{500}{\milli\second} vacuum buildup delay ($T_{\text{vacuum}} = \SI{500}{\milli\second}$) before lifting, and a \SI{400}{\milli\second} venting delay ($T_{\text{release}} = \SI{400}{\milli\second}$) at the drop location.

\subsection{Offline Speech Recognition Pipeline}
Voice commands are processed locally using the Vosk ASR engine with the lightweight \texttt{vosk-model-small-en-us} model sampled at \SI{16}{\kilo\hertz}. To prevent misrecognition in noisy environments, we use phonetic alias dictionaries: red maps to $\{\text{red, right, read, rad}\}$, yellow maps to $\{\text{yellow, yeah, yell, hello}\}$, and blue maps to $\{\text{blue, do, woah}\}$. Validated intents are published as JSON strings to the \texttt{/voice\_command} topic.

The 2D planar homography matrix $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ maps camera pixels $[u, v, 1]^T$ to table world coordinates $[X_w, Y_w, 1]^T$:
\begin{equation}
    s \begin{bmatrix} X_w \\ Y_w \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \mathbf{H} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} h_{11} & h_{12} & h_{13} \\ h_{21} & h_{22} & h_{23} \\ h_{31} & h_{32} & h_{33} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 1 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

The matrix $\mathbf{H}$ is solved using Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) with RANSAC outlier rejection across the 12 ArUco marker pairs:
\begin{equation}
    X_w = \frac{h_{11}u + h_{12}v + h_{13}}{h_{31}u + h_{32}v + h_{33}}, \quad Y_w = \frac{h_{21}u + h_{22}v + h_{23}}{h_{31}u + h_{32}v + h_{33}}
\end{equation}

Target cubes are segmented in the HSV color space, filtered morphologically, and their centroid $(\bar{u}, \bar{v})$ is computed using spatial image moments:
\begin{equation}
    \bar{u} = \frac{M_{10}}{M_{00}}, \quad \bar{v} = \frac{M_{01}}{M_{00}}
\end{equation}
The resulting centroid is projected through $\mathbf{H}$ to obtain the physical Cartesian pick coordinates $(X_{\text{pick}}, Y_{\text{pick}}, Z_{\text{pick}})$.

\subsection{Motion Planning and Execution}
Motion planning is performed in MoveIt~2 using the Pilz Industrial Motion Planner. Point-to-point (PTP) motions are used for fast transit between resting poses and the approach waypoint ($Z_{\text{approach}} = Z_{\text{pick}} + \SI{120}{\milli\meter}$). Linear (LIN) motions are used for straight vertical descent to the contact plane ($Z_{\text{pick}}$) and straight vertical retraction, preventing collisions with adjacent objects. The tool orientation is constrained pointing vertically downward:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{q}_{\text{ori}} = \left[ q_x = 0.0, \, q_y = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}, \, q_z = 0.0, \, q_w = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \right]^T
\end{equation}

\section{Automated Benchmarking Workflow}
\label{sec:benchmark_workflow}

To replace manual operator testing, we developed an automated benchmark runner (\texttt{auto\_benchmark\_runner.py}) and a telemetry logger (\texttt{benchmark\_logger.py}). The runner coordinates the entire cycle: (1) queries the vision service for target coordinates, (2) pre-validates workspace boundary safety, (3) emits \texttt{/benchmark\_run\_start}, (4) triggers \texttt{/voice\_command}, (5) monitors \texttt{/gripper\_cmd} and \texttt{/joint\_states}, (6) records position error at contact, and (7) emits \texttt{/benchmark\_run\_end} to log all metrics into \texttt{benchmark\_results.csv}.

\subsection{Physical Observations and Discussion}
Across all physical validation trials, the 12-marker ArUco homography localized target cubes with an average Cartesian error of \SI{3.18}{\milli\meter}. Because this error is well within the \SI{8.0}{\milli\meter} radius of the suction cup, the vacuum gripper achieved an airtight seal on every run. In Run 5, a minor gripper offset triggered the coordinator's built-in re-grasping recovery, demonstrating the system's ability to recover from small position disturbances.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper demonstrated an end-to-end, deterministic multimodal manipulation framework and an automated benchmarking runner for color-coded material handling using ROS~2 and an industrial KUKA KR6 R900-2 manipulator. By combining offline Vosk speech recognition, 12-marker ArUco homography, and MoveIt~2 Pilz motion planning over the KUKA Ethernet KRL Interface (EKI), the system achieves responsive hands-free manipulation with an average decision latency of \SI{1.26}{\second} and an average positioning error of \SI{3.18}{\milli\meter} on physical hardware. The automated benchmarking pipeline eliminates manual operator overhead, logging all tracking errors and cycle times directly to CSV.

Future work will extend the vision system to 6-DOF RGB-D point cloud pose estimation for complex 3D objects and explore on-device Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models for unconstrained natural language instructions.

\subsection*{Note on Present Dataset and Ongoing Multi-Condition Benchmarking}
The empirical metrics presented in Table~\ref{tab:latency_breakdown} and Table~\ref{tab:benchmark_summary} of this manuscript reflect the initial physical validation stage (Runs 1 to 5) conducted on the physical KUKA KR6 R900-2 Agilus industrial manipulator at Asia University. These initial trials successfully verified the core architectural integration: (1) low-latency local speech recognition via Vosk, (2) reliable planar homography coordinate mapping from the 12-marker ArUco constellation, (3) deterministic linear and point-to-point motion execution via MoveIt~2 Pilz planner, and (4) bidirectional telemetry exchange with the KUKA KRC4 controller over the Ethernet KRL Interface (EKI) on port 54600.
